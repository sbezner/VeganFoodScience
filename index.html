Why this course

Most plant-based cooking advice is folklore.
This is the science underneath it.

The plant-based food world moves fast — but the chemistry is timeless. Learn the principles once, and you'll understand every new product, ingredient, and technique that follows.

⚗️

First principles, not recipes

Why does aquafaba whip? What gives tempeh its bite? You'll learn the mechanisms — proteins, gels, emulsions, microbes — so you can predict, troubleshoot, and invent.

🧪

Hands-on in your kitchen

Every module has a lab built around equipment most home cooks already own. A scale, a thermometer, a blender — and 25 experiments later, you'll feel the science.

🌱

From the legume to the lab-grown

Cover the basics — beans, grains, seeds — and the frontier: precision fermentation, mycelium, 3D-printed meat alternatives. The whole landscape of vegan food, from kitchen to bioreactor.

📚

Free, open, and remixable

Released under Creative Commons. Fork it, translate it, teach it. The source lives on GitHub — every diagram, every quiz, every word.

The journey

Twelve modules, one continuous arc

Start with the chemistry of food itself, build up through proteins, fats, and fermentation, then synthesize everything into your own plant-based product.

A note from the course

Cooking is chemistry you can eat.

This course was built for the curious — for the cook who wonders why their cashew cheese tastes a little flat, the food entrepreneur trying to understand a spec sheet, the scientist who wants to actually taste the molecules they read about.

Plant-based food is one of the most interesting frontiers in modern science. It sits at the crossroads of biochemistry, ecology, sensory science, and craft. We'll treat it with the seriousness it deserves — and the joy it inspires.
